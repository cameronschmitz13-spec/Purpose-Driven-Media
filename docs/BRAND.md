# PDM Brand

## Name
Purpose Driven Media

## Core positioning
Preferred master line:

**Get Found, Understood, and Connected.**

Long-form organization line:

**Helping Organizations get Found, understood, and connected online.**

Audience-specific approved variants:

- **Businesses:** Helping businesses get discovered, trusted, and chosen online.
- **Organizations:** Helping Organizations get Found, understood, and connected online.
- **Ministries:** Helping Ministries get Found, understood, and connected online.
- **Non-Profits:** Helping Non-Profits get Found, understood, and connected online.

Use the organization version as the broad/default brand lockup unless the page is intentionally audience-specific.

## Product brand architecture

### Master company
**Purpose Driven Media (PDM)**

### Master promise
**Get Found, Understood, and Connected.**

### Flagship diagnostic
**PDM 360° Visibility Screening**

Preferred supporting line:

**See your organization from every angle people use to find, understand, trust, and connect with you.**

The 360° concept communicates breadth. It does not replace the existing Visibility Screening architecture.

Internally, the canonical scoring/rubric architecture remains the Visibility Screening and versioned rubrics such as `visibility-v1`.

### Audience configurations
Use the same product family and UX shell:

- **PDM 360° Business Visibility Screening**
- **PDM 360° Non-Profit Visibility Screening**
- **PDM 360° Faith & Ministry Visibility Screening**
- **PDM 360° Organization Visibility Screening**

These are configurations of one diagnostic product, not separate software products.

### Paid intelligence layer
**PDM 360° Advisor**

Customer-facing chat CTA:
**Ask PDM 360°**

Preferred supporting line:

**Your screening does not end with a score. Ask questions, test ideas, and turn your results into clear next moves with an advisor that already understands your organization.**

The Advisor is a PDM product. Its voice should be direct, practical, numbers-aware, evidence-backed, and constraint-first.

Do not market it as an imitation of, endorsement by, or licensed version of Alex Hormozi or any other public business personality.

The Advisor is premium and must be unlocked through a valid server-side entitlement tied to the user's authorized screening/report or qualifying plan.

See `docs/PDM_360_ADVISOR.md` for AI, verification, access-control, and sector-behavior requirements.

### Approved marketing shorthand
When space is limited, acceptable forms include:
- **PDM 360°**
- **360° Visibility Screening**
- **Your PDM 360° Report**

Do not use shorthand in a way that implies a separate scoring engine.

### Naming boundaries
Do not rename the score to:
- Growth Score
- Business Health Score
- Success Score
- Opportunity Score

The score measures visibility performance under the documented rubric and should remain a **Visibility Score**.

Growth, market, demographic, competitive, and community information may appear as contextual intelligence around the score without being silently folded into it.

## 360° narrative framework

Use 360° to explain that PDM looks beyond a website alone.

The customer story should connect directly to the master brand:

### FOUND
Can people discover the organization accurately and consistently?

Examples:
- identity
- search visibility
- local presence
- listings
- organic discovery

### UNDERSTOOD
Can people quickly understand who the organization is, what it offers, and why it matters?

Examples:
- message clarity
- mission/service clarity
- current information
- mobile experience
- trust signals

### CONNECTED
Can people confidently take the right next step?

Examples:
- contact
- booking
- quote
- visit
- donate
- volunteer
- attend
- request help
- engage with content

The scoring architecture remains more detailed than these three marketing buckets. Do not collapse the rubric into only three scored categories unless a future version explicitly documents that change.

## Market & Community Intelligence
The 360° report may include an intelligence layer around the Visibility Score, such as:
- geographic context
- demographics
- market/community characteristics
- nearby organizations
- competitors or comparable organizations
- service-area context
- relevant Census/ACS estimates
- documented opportunity observations

This intelligence layer should help answer:

**What is happening around this organization that should influence its visibility strategy?**

It is contextual intelligence, not automatically score-bearing evidence.

Any scored use must be explicitly defined in the rubric and pass the normal evidence/identity requirements.

## Primary logo system

The canonical visual mark uses:

- two forward-facing gold chevrons
- large navy `PDM` initials for compact/square usage
- full wordmark `Purpose Driven Media` for horizontal lockups
- a thin gold vertical divider before the audience tagline in full horizontal lockups

Do **not** replace the chevrons with leaves, plants, mountains, arrows from another icon family, or generic technology marks.

Do not redraw or alter the proportions of the chevrons when the supplied brand asset is available.

## Brand colors

Colors sampled directly from the supplied master assets:

- **PDM Navy:** `#0A2B4F` — RGB 10, 43, 79
- **PDM Gold:** `#C99A22` — RGB 201, 154, 34
- **Warm Gold Highlight:** approximately `#CFAA2B` — RGB 207, 170, 43
- **Warm Off-White:** approximately `#FBFAF9`

Use navy and gold as the dominant identity. Brighter supporting colors are allowed in campaign creative, charts, and status indicators, but the PDM identity should remain immediately recognizable.

## Logo usage

### Preferred
- white or warm off-white background
- generous clear space around the mark
- navy wordmark with gold chevrons
- supplied audience lockup when relevant

### Avoid
- stretching or squashing the logo
- changing chevron count
- rotating the chevrons
- recoloring the primary mark without an approved alternate
- adding shadows, bevels, glow effects, or decorative icons to the logo itself
- placing the logo over visually noisy imagery without sufficient contrast
- recreating the mark with AI image generation when the actual supplied brand asset is available

## Website guidance

For the main PDM website, prefer the **Organizations** horizontal lockup unless a specific landing page targets businesses, ministries, or non-profits.

The compact `>> PDM` mark is appropriate for:
- favicon/app-icon concepts
- mobile header
- social avatar
- small UI areas

The full `>> Purpose Driven Media | Helping …` lockup is appropriate for:
- desktop header
- footer
- formal brand presentation
- campaign headers where horizontal space allows

### Website screening language
Preferred homepage/product language:

**PDM 360° Visibility Screening**

**See your organization from every angle.**

Supporting copy should explain the promise rather than rely on “360°” alone.

Preferred CTA:
**Start My 360° Visibility Screening**

Preferred secondary CTA:
**See a Sample Report**

Avoid turning “360°” into decorative jargon. It should consistently communicate that PDM evaluates the full public visibility journey across discovery, understanding, trust, and next-step readiness.

## Voice
Direct, credible, practical, specific, and business-owner friendly.

Avoid filler and hype such as:
- game-changing
- unleash
- elevate
- revolutionary
- cutting-edge
- in today's fast-paced world

Specific beats clever.

## Copy consistency

Capitalize the three strategic words when used as the core brand framework:

**Found, Understood, and Connected**

When using the Businesses variant, preserve its distinct approved language:

**discovered, trusted, and chosen**

Do not silently mix the two frameworks in the same lockup.
